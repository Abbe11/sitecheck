Mobile-money & AI scam defense · Kenya

Nobody legitimate needs your PIN, your code, or your money right now.

Scammers use fake M-PESA messages, calls pretending to be Safaricom, and AI that clones a loved one's voice. They all rely on one trick: panicking you into acting before you can check. Here is how to stay safe — and share it with the people you love.

The one rule that beats them all

Stop. Verify on your own, using a number you already trust. Never act on a single urgent call or SMS.

A real relative, bank, or Safaricom will let you hang up and check. A scammer needs you to move now. That pressure is the scam — every time.

The scams hitting Kenya right now

Each one, how it works, and what to do instead.

Fake reversal

"Money was sent to you by mistake — please send it back."

You get an SMS that looks like an M-PESA "received" message, then a call begging you to reverse it. The SMS is fake — no money actually came in.

Do this: Check your real balance yourself in the M-PESA app or menu — don't trust the SMS they point to. A genuine wrong-send is reversed by Safaricom, never by you sending cash to a stranger. Report the number to 333.

Fake Safaricom call

"This is Safaricom customer care — press these codes / confirm your PIN."

A caller claims your line or M-PESA has a problem and tells you to enter USSD codes or share your PIN or OTP. Doing it blocks your line or sends your money to them.

Do this: Safaricom's genuine calls come only from 0722 000 000 — any other "Safaricom" number is a scam. They will never ask for your PIN or make you press codes. Hang up and call 100 yourself to check.

AI voice clone

"It's me — I'm in trouble, send money on M-PESA quickly."

AI can copy a relative's voice from a few seconds of audio (a voice note, a video online). The "child" or "friend" who calls in a panic may be a machine.

Do this: Hang up and call the real person back on their normal number. Ask your family safe word (make one below), or a question only they would know. A fake cannot answer.

SIM swap

Your line suddenly goes dead — and your M-PESA drains.

A fraudster tricks a shop into moving your number to their SIM, then resets your M-PESA and bank OTPs. The first sign is losing signal for no reason.

Do this: If your line dies unexpectedly, treat it as an emergency — contact Safaricom immediately (100) from another phone. Set a SIM PIN and an ID lock on your line so nobody can swap it without you.

Fake win

"Congratulations! You've won a promotion — pay a small fee to claim."

A prize you never entered, with a "release" or "activation" fee. You send the fee; the prize never exists.

Do this: You cannot win a promotion you never entered. Real prizes never need you to pay first. Ignore it and report the number to 333.

Golden rules

From Safaricom's own fraud-awareness guidance.

  1. Never share your PIN or any OTP/code — with anyone, ever. Safaricom and your bank will never ask.
  2. Never press USSD codes because a caller told you to — it can block your line or send your money.
  3. Genuine Safaricom calls come only from 0722 000 000. Any other "Safaricom" caller is a scam.
  4. Report scam numbers and fake SMS to 333. Call Safaricom yourself on 100 to verify anything.
  5. If you think you have been compromised, change your PIN immediately and contact Safaricom.

Set your family safe word

Agree on a word in person — never by text. If a "relative" calls in a panic asking for money, ask for it. A voice clone will not know it.
